knowledge. Each part builds on the one before, so it helps to read them in

## The idea: categories instead of folders

On a computer you keep files in folders, and each file sits in exactly one
folder. That forces a choice. Does the plane ticket for your summer trip go
in *Travel*, in *Japan*, or in *Receipts*? Whichever you pick, you'll one
day look for it in another.

categor.io is built on **OntoDAG**, which works differently. There are no
folders, only **categories**, and anything can sit in as many categories as
it belongs to. The plane ticket can be under *Japan trip* and under
*receipts* at the same time. It's still one ticket, not three copies.

Categories sit inside other categories too. *Japan trip* can be under
*Travel*. When you look at *Travel*, you see everything below it, however
deep: the trip, and the ticket inside the trip, even though you never filed
the ticket under *Travel* yourself.

One word matters throughout this guide: **below**. If A is below B, then A
is part of B, or a kind of B. A dog is below *animal*. Your ticket is below
*Japan trip*, which is below *Travel*. Everything on this site, sharing
included, comes down to what is below what.

## Part 1: Exploring, no account needed

Everyone can explore the **public vocabulary**: thousands of everyday
categories, from *toaster* to *photosynthesis*, already arranged for you.
They come in **packs**, collections on one subject.

### Step 1: Open a pack

On the home page you see a list of packs. **core** holds everyday things
(objects, people, places, events, documents). The others cover one field
each, such as physics, medicine, geography or economics.

Click **core**. You see its top categories, such as *physical-object*,
*information*, *event* and *place*. The small grey number next to a
category tells you how many categories are below it.

### Step 2: Walk up and down

Click **physical-object**, then **artifact**, then **device**. Each page
shows:

- **the category's name** at the top;
- **"under …"**: the categories it sits in. Click one to go up a level;
- **the list below**: the categories inside it. Click one to go down.

Now look for a category with two parents. Many things belong in more than
one place, and the "under" line shows all of them.

The **Home** link at the top of every page takes you back to the start.

### Step 3: See everything below, and narrow it down

At the bottom of a category's list you may see a link such as **All 1,203
below device →**. It lists everything below the category, however deep.

Above that list you'll find **Narrow by**, a row of suggestions, each with
a number. Click one, say *kitchen-appliance*, and the list shrinks to things
that are *both* devices *and* kitchen appliances. The number told you in
advance how many would remain. Only suggestions that actually narrow the
list are offered.

The heading now reads **device and kitchen-appliance**. Click the **×**
beside either word to drop it again.

### Step 4: Search

Type part of a name, such as *toast*, into **Find a category** at the top
of any page, and press Enter.

## Part 2: Your own things

### Step 5: Create an account

Click **Sign in**, then **Create an account**. Choose a username with 3 to
32 letters, digits, dots, dashes or underscores, and a password of at least
8 characters.

Your username gives you an **address**, such as **ada@categor.io**. It looks
like an email address, and works a little like one: people who know it can
share things with you (Part 4). Nobody else can take your username, ever,
even if you leave.

Everything you create is kept in your **store**. It is private: nobody else
can see anything in it unless you share it.

### Step 6: Add your first categories

Your **Home** page has a box called **Add a category** on the right. Type
*hobby* as the name, leave **Under** empty, and click **Add**. Do the same
for *work*.

These are now on your home page under **Yours**. Many people start with a
few big areas of life like these, one for each "side" of themselves.

### Step 7: File something under the public vocabulary

Say you have a dog called Rex. Add a category named *rex*, and in **Under**
type *dog*. As you type, the site suggests names; pick **dog**.

Rex is now below *dog*, and so also below *mammal*, *animal* and
*organism*: the public vocabulary already knows where dogs belong. Try
searching for *animal* and clicking **All … below animal**. Rex is in the
list.

Rex is still **yours alone**. Other people who look at *dog* see only the
public categories, never your Rex. Filing something under a public category
describes it for you; it doesn't publish it.

### Step 8: Put one thing in several places

Open **rex** (click it, or search for it). On the right, under **File rex
under**, type *hobby* and click **File**. The "under" line now reads *dog,
hobby*. Rex is in both, and still just one Rex.

### Step 9: Build inside your categories

Open **hobby**. Use **Add a category under this** to add *photos*, then
open *photos* and add *summer-2026* under it.

A handy trick for dates: in **Under** you can type a date category such as
*time(2026-08)* for August 2026. Later, looking at *time(2026)* shows
everything you filed anywhere in 2026, without your ever filing it under
the year.

### Step 10: Change your mind

- **Take something out of one place.** On its page, under **Take out from
  under**, click the **×** on the category it should leave. It stays in its
  other places. (**home ×** takes it off your home page.)
- **Remove a category.** Open **Remove …** at the bottom right. The
  category goes, but the things under it are not lost: they move up to its
  parents.
- **Undo.** Click **Store** at the top of the page. **Undo** takes back your
  last change, and **Redo** puts it back again. The **History** list shows
  every change you've made.

## Part 3: Sharing

### Step 11: Share a category with someone

Say your friend Bob has the address **bob@categor.io**, and you want him to
see your holiday photos.

1. Open your *photos* category.
2. Under **Share with**, type *bob* (the full address works too) and click
   **Share**.

Bob now gets *photos* and **everything below it**: *summer-2026*, and
anything you add there later. You don't need to share each photo. Filing a
photo under *photos* is enough.

The page now says **shared with @bob**.

> **Check the spelling.** If Bob's address is new in your store, the site
> reminds you to check it. The site never tells you whether an address
> exists, so that nobody can use it to find out who has an account. A
> misspelled share simply reaches no one.

### Step 12: What Bob sees, and what he doesn't

Bob sees *photos* and what is below it. He does **not** see:

- the other places you filed *photos* in. If it's also under your private
  *family*, *family* stays hidden;
- anything else in your store;
- the other people you've shared *photos* with.

And Bob sees nothing at all until he accepts you (Part 4).

### Step 13: Groups

To share with several people at once, make a **group**. A group is just a
category that you share with several people.

1. Add a category called *climbing-club*.
2. Share it with each member: *bob*, *carol*, *dan*.
3. File whatever the club should see under *climbing-club*.

Every member sees everything under *climbing-club*, and no member sees who
else is in the group. Only you, the owner, see the member list.

### Step 14: Groups inside groups (a company example)

Say Acme sets up its account like this:

- *employees*, shared with every employee;
- *employee-information* under *employees*, with the *handbook* under it;
- *sales*, shared with the sales team;
- *sales-leads* under *sales*.

Acme wants the sales team to see everything employees see as well. So Acme
files **employees under sales**. Everyone in sales now sees the handbook,
because it is below *employees*, which is below *sales*. Employees outside
sales still don't see *sales-leads*.

This is the opposite of how you might first picture it. Remember that below
means "part of": *employees* becomes part of what sales can see. More
access means more below you.

On Acme's own pages, people who get something through a group are listed
as **through a group**.

### Step 15: Stop sharing

Open the category and click the **×** on the person's name under **Take
out from under**.

If a change would stop someone seeing something, the site first shows a
page headed **This stops people seeing things**. It lists who would lose
what, and you choose **Go ahead** or **Cancel**. The same check runs when
you take something out of a group or remove a category, so nobody loses
access by accident.

## Part 4: Receiving

### Step 16: Requests

When someone shares with you for the first time, it doesn't appear straight
away. Your home page shows a **request** instead: **acme wants to share 3
things with you**, with no content. That protects you from things you never
asked for.

You have three choices:

- **Accept.** Pick where the sender should go in your store (**at the top**,
  or one of your categories, such as *work*), then click **Accept**.
- **Block.** You get no more requests from them, and nothing of theirs is
  shown.
- **Leave it.** The request stays until you decide.

The sender is never told what you chose.

### Step 17: Where shared things appear

Once you've accepted Acme:

- **On your home page, "Shared with you"** lists Acme with the number of
  things it shares.
- **Acme's page** (click **@acme**) shows **What acme shares with you** and
  **What you share with acme**.
- **Where you filed Acme**, for example under *work*. Your *work* category
  then leads to everything Acme shares with you.
- **Mixed into the vocabulary.** If Acme's handbook is filed under the
  public *document*, you'll see it there too when you look at *document*,
  marked with a small **acme** label. Only you, and others Acme shared it
  with, see it there.

Shared things belong to their owner, so you can read them but not change
them.

If you share something with someone yourself, you've accepted them too:
their shares to you appear without a request.

### Step 18: Blocking and unblocking

Open the person's page (click their name) and click **Block …**. To undo
it, open the same page and click **Unblock**. **Remove from your store**
goes further: you stop accepting their shares, *and* stop sharing with
them.

### Step 19: An address for one part of your life

You can have extra addresses, each tied to one of your categories. On your
home page, under **Your addresses**, fill in **New address, for one
category**: type *work*, choose your *work* category, and click
**Create**. You now have **ada+work@categor.io**.

Anything shared with *ada+work* arrives in your *work* category. Give that
address to colleagues, and keep *ada* for friends.

Each address has its own setting for requests. Open the address (click it
under **Your addresses**) and choose under **Requests at this address**:

- **shown**: strangers can send you requests here;
- **hidden: accepted senders only**: only people you've already accepted
  can reach you here; others are silently ignored;
- **as the account**: follow the setting of your main address.

Requests are shown by default.

## Part 5: Your store as a whole

Click **Store** at the top of any page.

- **Export** downloads your whole store as one file (*ada.od*). It's a plain
  text file in OntoDAG's own format, which you can keep as a backup. People
  who use OntoDAG's command-line tool, `odag`, can open it directly. Under
  **Also include whole packs** you can add complete packs to the file, for
  example all of biology to work with on your own computer. The packs you
  already use are listed first. This changes only the file, not your store.
- **Import** adds the contents of such a file to your store. First click
  **Preview**. You see what would be added, and whether the file would share
  anything with anyone, *before* anything changes. Then click **Merge into
  my store**, or cancel.
- **History** lists every change you've made, newest first, with **Undo**
  and **Redo**.

## Part 6: Pictures and the console

### Step 20: See a category as a picture

Below the name of every category are three links: **List**, **Picture**
and **As a command**. Click **Picture**. The category appears in the
middle, highlighted, with the categories it sits in on one side and the
ones inside it on the other. Every box is a link, so you can walk around
by clicking boxes.

A picture only ever shows what you can see yourself. On something shared
with you, the owner's private categories don't appear, just as they don't
appear in the list.

### Step 21: Ask the same thing as a command

OntoDAG has its own short command language. The **Console** link at the
top of every page opens it. Type a command after **odag ▸** and click
**Run**:

- `get device kitchen-appliance` lists what is both a device and a kitchen
  appliance;
- `count animal` says how many categories are below *animal*;
- `below rex animal` answers *true* or *false*: is Rex below *animal*?
- `canon weight(3000g)` shows how a value is stored: *weight(3kg)*.

You don't need to learn this. Every page has an **As a command** link that
opens the console with that page's question already typed in, so you can
pick it up by looking.

When you're signed in, choose where commands run: **Your store** or
**Public vocabulary**. In your store you can also change things:

- `put holiday-photos` adds a category, and `put rex hobby` files Rex
  under *hobby*;
- `put photos bob@categor.io` shares *photos* with Bob, exactly like the
  Share button;
- `remove photos` removes a category;
- `undo` takes back your last change, and `history` lists your changes;
- `pack biology --show` lists everything in the biology pack.

As on the pages, if a command would stop someone seeing something, the
console asks first and waits for you to click **Run anyway**.

Some OntoDAG commands never run here, because they would read or write
files on the site's computer or change its settings. The list below the
console shows every command, and why any that can't run here are
unavailable.

## Good to know

**Public words are public categories.** If you add a category with a name
the vocabulary already has, such as *work*, *hobby* or *family*, you get
the public one. What you file under it is still private. On your own
categories the site lists your things first; the public subcategories are
behind a link such as **11 public categories under work**.

**Every pack is ready to use.** You never need to add a pack before using
it. File your things under *gene* or *enzyme* (biology), *exoplanet*
(space) or *antiviral* (medicine) straight away, as with *dog*.

**Nobody can search for you.** The site never lists accounts. To share with
someone you need their address, the way you need an email address.

**Shares to addresses that don't exist go nowhere, for good.** If you share
with an address nobody has registered, it reaches no one. That stays true
even if someone registers that name later: whatever was filed before they
existed is never given to them.

**Senders learn nothing about you.** They can't see your store. They don't
learn whether you accepted, blocked or ignored them.

**Everything is undoable.** Every change goes into your History, and
**Undo** takes it back.

## Words used on the site

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Category** | Anything with a name: a topic, a thing, a group of people. Categories can sit inside other categories. |
| **Below** | Part of, or a kind of. Rex is below *dog*; the handbook is below *employee-information*. |
| **Under** | The categories something sits in (its "parents"). |
| **Public vocabulary** | The categories everyone can see, arranged in advance. |
| **Pack** | A collection of public categories on one subject. |
| **Store** | Everything that's yours: your categories and what you've filed. Private unless you share. |
| **Address** | *name@categor.io*. Share with it to reach someone; yours lets others reach you. |
| **Share** | File a category under someone's address. They get it and everything below it. |
| **Group** | A category shared with several people. |
| **Request** | A first share from someone you haven't accepted yet: their name and a count, nothing more. |
| **Accept** | File the sender in your store, so that what they share appears. |
| **Block** | Hide everything from a sender, including their requests. |
| **Picture** | A drawing of a category with what it sits in and what is inside it. |
| **Console** | A place to type OntoDAG commands, such as `get dog`, instead of clicking. |
| **OntoDAG** | The system underneath: categories that can have several parents. The name comes from "ontology" (an arrangement of categories) and "DAG" (a network with no loops). |
